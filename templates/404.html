{% extends "base.html" %} {% comment %} What a reader lands on when a URL answers nothing. Before this there was no 404.html and no handler404 anywhere, so Django's stock page answered: no viewport meta (which lays it out at Chrome's 980px fallback on a phone, clipping its own sentence mid-word), no wordmark, and no way back into the site. That was tolerable while a 404 meant a typo. It is not now: an audience that excludes the reader answers 404 by design, on the list page, on the AP arm of a list face, and on the Save door - so this is a common page, not a rare one. It says nothing but the number, and that is deliberate rather than spare. Both "it is gone" and "you may not see it" land here, and naming either would hand back the very fact the 404 was chosen over a 403 to withhold. The numeral wears the wordmark's tube treatment (see the .neon-404 rules) so the page is recognisably the site's before a word of it is read. {% endcomment %} {% block title %}404{% endblock %} {% block main_class %} notfound-page{% endblock %} {% block content %}
