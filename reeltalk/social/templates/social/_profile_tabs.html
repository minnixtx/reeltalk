{% comment %} The tab strip the profile and films pages share: it sits above the body, like every tabbed surface the site has - the strip says where you are, the body below it is what you are looking at. The caller names the active tab: ``with profile_tab="reviews"`` (or "films"). Reviews is the profile's own body (D1), so its tab points at the profile URL itself - there is no second URL to page. The strip is always shown, never hidden for being empty (D5); the empty state in the body is what an empty list looks like. Reviews and Films are the strip on every account. Lists is the strip on somebody ELSE's pages only, because that is the one place it is the only way there: the sidebar and the phone bar both carry a Lists row, and both always point at the viewer's own lists, so on your own page the tab is a duplicate of a row the menu already shows and on another member's page nothing else reaches their lists. Keying the row on the viewer rather than on the page is what keeps the strip identical between the pages it spans - a rule keyed on the page would let the row appear on one tab and vanish on the next click, which is what this used to do. Standing on the lists page forces the row even at zero lists: a strip that hides the tab you are standing on says you are nowhere, and the page is reachable by URL whatever its count. The lists page only includes this partial when it is not your own, so that clause can never light the row on your own pages, where the My Lists / Saved Lists row below is what says where you are. Where the row does apply it stays hidden when the member has made no lists - R138 decision 3 - rather than shown with an empty state, because a tab that leads nowhere is worse than one that appears when there is something behind it. ``list_count`` is the whole decision and the view makes it via ``_profile_head_context``; the template never reaches for the database. ``is_self`` comes from the same builder, and an anonymous visitor is not self - they get no menu at all, so for them the row is the only way too. {% endcomment %}
